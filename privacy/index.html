Privacy Policy

Your phone keeps what it knows.

Pausely has no account, and everything it records about you stays on your device. Two things leave it: what Meta's SDK reports so we can measure our ads, and — only if you switch it on and OpenAI writes it — what you type into the AI reflection. This page is about exactly where those lines are.

The short version

Pausely has no account. Everything it records — which apps you protect, how long you spend in them, your pauses, your journal — is written to your device and stays there. Pausely cannot even read which apps you chose; Apple's Screen Time keeps that.

Two things leave your device, and this page lists every part of both:

  • Meta's SDK, from Pausely 1.3, so we can tell which of our ads on Facebook and Instagram brought people to the app. It tells Meta when Pausely is installed or opened and when a plan is bought, with the kind of device and an identifier it makes up for this install. It never learns which apps you protect, how you use them or anything you write, and because Pausely never asks to track you, Meta gets no advertising identifier. Details.
  • The AI reflection, which is off until you switch it on. On an iPhone with Apple Intelligence it can be written on the phone itself, and then nothing leaves. When OpenAI writes it, the words you type into that one pause go to Pausely's server and on to OpenAI, with four small facts about the moment. Details.
Your journal never leaves your device. Your check-ins never leave. The minutes you spend in other apps never leave. Which apps you chose never leaves — Pausely cannot read that even on your own device.

In the terms of Apple's privacy labels: no tracking; identifiers, usage data, purchases, diagnostics and other data, collected by Meta's SDK to measure our ads; and user content, not linked to you, used only to make the AI reflection work. This policy applies to the Pausely app for iPhone and iPad, published by TOPSOL, and to this website.

What we collect

"Collect", in Apple's sense and in ours, means transmitting data off the device and keeping it somewhere that is not transient. By that measure Pausely collects what Meta's SDK reports so we can measure our ads, and — only if you switch on the AI reflection and OpenAI writes it — the words you type into that one screen. Nothing else.

What stays on your device

Pausely stores the following in its own private storage on your iPhone or iPad, protected by your device's passcode and encryption. None of it is uploaded, backed up by us, or visible to us.

  • Your app selection — the apps you protect, and any you chose for Time banners, as opaque Screen Time tokens Pausely cannot read (see below).
  • Your rules and schedules — the names you give them, their hours and their pause.
  • Pause outcomes — that a pause was shown, when, and whether you walked away, went in, or chose an alternative. This is what the progress screen, the streak and the chart are made of.
  • Time in the apps you chose — minutes per day, as iOS counts them, for your daily goal, your limits, the short-video estimate and Time banners. Kept for up to sixty days.
  • What you write — journal entries, the words you type on the pause screen, mood check-ins after a session, and the names of custom alternatives.
  • Settings — your chosen pause, strict-mode state, alternatives, the apps you marked as mostly short videos, your language, and preferences.
  • Your Premium entitlement — a tier and an expiry date, so the app knows what to unlock.

If you use iCloud Backup or an encrypted local backup, Apple may include Pausely's data in that backup under your own Apple ID and Apple's privacy terms. We have no access to it.

Screen Time: your apps and your time

Pausely is built on Apple's Screen Time framework (Family Controls, Managed Settings and Device Activity). You grant it access once, through Apple's own prompt, and can revoke it at any time in Settings → Screen Time.

When you pick apps, Apple's picker returns tokens: values that identify the app to iOS but mean nothing to Pausely and nothing outside your device. Pausely stores those tokens so it can ask iOS to shield the apps you chose, and it never receives a bundle identifier. Where it needs to compare tokens between its own components it compares one-way hashes of them, never the tokens themselves. Apart from the sealed screen described below, the one place it can read an app's name is the block screen: iOS lets it see the name of the app you are standing in front of, so that the pause — and the AI reflection, if you use it — can say "Instagram" rather than "an app". That name stays on your device, except in the AI reflection's request when OpenAI writes it.

How long you spend in them

For your daily goal, your limits, the short-video estimate and Time banners, iOS tells Pausely each time the apps you chose pass another few minutes of use today. Pausely writes down minutes per day — on your device, never sent anywhere, and erased by Settings → Your data → Reset all data.

The Time per app screen, and the minutes under an app's icon on the pause screen, are drawn by an extension Apple runs in a sealed sandbox. It sees app names and exact minutes, but it cannot use the network or hand anything back, so the rest of Pausely never learns them and nothing on that screen is kept.

The short-video figure is an estimate and always says "about": the minutes in the apps you marked, divided by the length you chose for one video. Pausely never sees a video, or anything else on your screen.

Pausely does not observe your browsing, your messages, or what you do inside any app. iOS itself draws the block screen and enforces the shield. Besides the minutes above, Pausely learns only that its own pause was shown and what you chose on it.

Meta's SDK — measuring our ads

Pausely runs ads on Facebook and Instagram. From Pausely 1.3, the app includes Meta's software development kit (SDK) so we can tell which of those ads brought people to Pausely. It is the one piece of someone else's code in the app. It runs only inside the app itself — never in the parts that draw the block screen, count your time or draw the widgets — and Pausely never hands it anything of its own.

What it sends to Meta

  • That Pausely was installed, and each time it is opened and for how long.
  • That a plan was bought, a free trial started or a subscription renewed — which plan, its price and its currency — as it notices on your device.
  • Technical details such as the kind of device, its iOS version, its language and its time zone.
  • An identifier the SDK makes up for this install of Pausely. It is not your name, your Apple ID or your device's advertising identifier.
  • Crash reports from Meta's own code.
  • If you open Pausely from one of its ads, the link the ad opened it with.

Like any server, Meta's also sees the IP address these arrive from.

What it never gets

  • Which apps you protect, or how long you spend in them.
  • Your pauses, rules, wins, journal and check-ins, or anything you type — including what you type into the AI reflection.
  • Any event of Pausely's own. The SDK reports only what is listed above, which it notices by itself; Pausely never tells it anything.
  • Your advertising identifier. Pausely never asks for permission to track you, so iOS does not hand it out, and the SDK tells Meta that tracking is not allowed.

The SDK's features that would read what is on your screen — what you type into a text field, the labels of the buttons you tap, the layout of a screen — are switched off for Pausely, in the app and in Meta's settings, and they stay off.

Meta keeps what it receives under Meta's Privacy Policy, which says how long it is kept and how to exercise your rights with Meta. The identifier lives in Pausely's storage on your device: deleting the app deletes it, and a new install starts with a new one.

The AI reflection — only if you switch it on

This is an optional Premium feature. It is off when you install Pausely and stays off until you switch it on, and switching it off again stops all of it.

With it on, one pause becomes a short exchange: you type why you are opening the app, and a question comes back rather than a lecture. Once a week it also writes a short note about how your week went.

Who writes it

On an iPhone with Apple Intelligence, Apple's model can write the question and the weekly note on the phone itself. Then nothing listed below leaves your device: no request is made, and nothing is kept. That is how it starts wherever Apple Intelligence is available. Otherwise — or if you choose it at Settings → AI reflection → Written by — OpenAI writes it, and the rest of this section applies. If you chose Apple Intelligence and it cannot answer, the pause shows its plain question instead: your words never go somewhere you decided they should not.

What is sent when OpenAI writes it

  • The display name of the app you are opening — the name already on your screen at that moment.
  • Three numbers: how many times you have opened it today, the time on the clock, and how long your streak is.
  • The words you type into that screen, in that moment.

The weekly note sends less: counts for the week and nothing else. No app names appear in it at all.

What is never sent

  • Your journal. It is the one place you write for nobody, and it never leaves the device. On an iPhone with Apple Intelligence, Pausely can write a few lines about your week's entries — on the phone, and nowhere else.
  • Your check-ins, moods, or any note attached to them.
  • Your app selection — which apps you protect, or how many — and never a Screen Time token, a token hash or a bundle identifier.
  • How long you spend in any app.
  • Your rules, schedules or strict-mode state.
  • Anything that identifies you. There is no account, no user ID and no advertising identifier to send. The request carries an Apple App Attest assertion, which tells our server that it is talking to a genuine copy of Pausely — it is scoped to the app, not to you, and does not follow you between requests.

Where it goes, and how long it is kept

Your words go to Pausely's own server, and on to OpenAI, which runs the model. Pausely never calls a model vendor's API from your device, and there is no API key inside the app to be extracted.

Pausely's server stores nothing per request — no prompt, no reply, no context. To stop runaway use it keeps one number per device per day — how many reflections that device asked for, under a random device key — and deletes it after two days. Its logs record the shape of a request (a timestamp, how long it took, whether it succeeded) and never its contents. OpenAI keeps inputs and outputs for up to 30 days in its abuse-monitoring logs, and may examine them to check for abuse. OpenAI does not use any of it to train its models.

Earlier versions of Pausely send the same words to Google instead, which keeps inputs and outputs for up to 24 hours, may examine prompts to check for abuse, and does not use them to train its models.

Settings → AI reflection → What is sent lists every field in the app itself, in the same words as this page.

Purchases

Premium is sold through Apple's App Store using StoreKit. The transaction is between you and Apple, under Apple's privacy policy. Apple tells Pausely whether a purchase is active — nothing else — and Pausely tells Apple nothing about how you use the app. No usage data, no journal, no app selection and no identifier of ours travels with a purchase. Separately, Meta's SDK notices purchases on your device and reports them so we can measure our ads — see Meta's SDK.

Notifications, widgets and the Lock Screen

Notifications are optional and are made on your device. Pausely uses them to hand you from the block screen into the pause, and to ask how it went when the time for an alternative you chose — a walk, a book — is up. Two more come only if you turn them on: a reminder once a day shortly before your hardest hour, and Time banners (Premium), which tell you every few minutes how long you have spent today in apps you chose. A time banner never names the app — it says "this app" — because notifications also appear on the Lock Screen. No notification is sent from a server: there is no push server and no marketing.

Widgets and Live Activities show counts, minutes and dates — today's wins, your streak, time reclaimed, your time today, the short-video estimate, and how long an access window has left. They deliberately do not have Screen Time access and never see which apps you blocked. A Lock Screen countdown names the rule you wrote ("Bedtime"), never the app, because a Lock Screen is readable by anyone holding the device. While an access window is open, the Dynamic Island also counts how long you have spent in that app through Pausely today; the Lock Screen does not show it.

Third parties

Pausely contains one piece of third-party code: Meta's SDK, described above. There is no other analytics, advertising or crash-reporting code in the app. These parties are involved, and only these:

  • Apple operates the App Store, processes purchases, enforces the Screen Time shield, runs Apple Intelligence on your iPhone if the AI reflection uses it, and — if you have opted in to sharing with developers — may share crash logs with us under its own policy.
  • Meta receives what its SDK reports so we can measure our ads, and nothing else.
  • Cloudflare hosts Pausely's server. When OpenAI writes the AI reflection, your words pass through it on their way to the model, and it stores none of them.
  • OpenAI runs the model behind the AI reflection when it writes it, and only for as long as you have that feature switched on. It receives what is listed above and nothing else, and it does not use it to train its models. Earlier versions of the app use Google for this instead.

We do not sell or rent personal data to anyone, and we share nothing with data brokers.

Deleting your data

Everything Pausely holds is erasable from inside the app, without asking us:

Settings → Your data → Reset all data
Empties every pause, win, rule, journal entry and check-in, takes the shields down, withdraws the schedules and starts you at the beginning. It refuses while strict mode is running — the one commitment you asked the app to hold you to — and it does not touch your subscription, which belongs to your Apple ID.

Deleting the app removes all of its data from the device as well, including the identifier Meta's SDK made up for it. On our side there is nothing to delete: our server keeps no record of a request once it has answered it. What Meta's SDK has already sent is held by Meta, under Meta's Privacy Policy. You are welcome to write to us anyway — see the Your data page.

Your rights

Wherever you live — including under the GDPR, the UK GDPR and the CCPA/CPRA — you have rights to access, correct, export, restrict, object to and erase personal data a company holds about you, and to complain to a supervisory authority. Pausely holds no profile of you and keeps nothing after a reflection is answered, so there is nothing on our side to produce or delete; the data on your device is already in your hands, and the app's own controls exercise every one of those rights for you. What Meta's SDK sent is held by Meta, and Meta's Privacy Policy explains how to exercise your rights there. If you have a question, or believe we hold something this page does not describe, write to topsol.org@gmail.com and we will respond.

Children

Pausely is a self-directed tool for the person using the device. It is not a parental-control product and does not manage another person's device. It is not directed at children under 13 (or the age of digital consent where you live), and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Changes to this policy

We may update this policy as the app changes. The date at the top moves when we do, and material changes will be noted in the app's release notes. The most recent substantive change came with Pausely 1.3: Meta's SDK, which measures our ads, and new time features — Time per app, Time banners and the short-video estimate — which, like the daily goal, count on your device how long you spend in the apps you choose. Before that, the AI reflection moved from Google to OpenAI, and gained the choice of Apple Intelligence on your iPhone. Continued use of Pausely after a change means you accept the revised policy.
