How to stop doomscrolling on iPhone: 7 things that work

Doomscrolling is rarely a decision. Your thumb opens the app before you have noticed, and twenty minutes go by. So the fixes that work are the ones that interrupt the opening, not the ones that ask you to be stronger. Here are seven, lightest first.

By the Pausely team · Updated 27 November 2026 · 7 min read

1. Move the app off your first Home Screen

The cheapest fix there is. Most opens start with a thumb going to the place the icon has always been.

  1. Press and hold the app's icon and tap Edit Home Screen.
  2. Drag it to your last page, or tap the minus and choose Remove from Home Screen. The app stays on the phone, in the App Library.

Now opening it means searching for it by name, which is a small decision. Where it stops working: your thumb learns the new route within a week or two.

2. Turn off the notifications that pull you in

A lot of scrolling starts with a notification about something that could have waited.

  1. Open Settings, tap Notifications, then the app.
  2. Turn off Allow Notifications. If that is too much, keep them on and turn off Lock Screen and Banners, so they wait quietly in Notification Center.

If you still want to hear from the app once or twice a day, Scheduled Summary on the same screen delivers a batch at times you choose.

3. Make the screen less interesting

Feeds are designed around colour. In grey they are noticeably duller, and that is the point.

  1. Open Settings, then Accessibility, then Display & Text Size.
  2. Tap Color Filters, turn it on and choose Grayscale.

To switch it quickly, go to Accessibility, then Accessibility Shortcut, and tick Color Filters. Three clicks of the side button now turn grey on and off. Where it stops working: photos and maps are worse in grey too, so most people end up turning it on only in the evening.

4. Set a daily limit, and close the escape hatch

Screen Time can give an app a daily allowance.

  1. Open Settings and tap Screen Time. If it has never been set up, turn on App & Website Activity first.
  2. Tap App Limits, then Add Limit, and tick the apps.
  3. Tap Next, set the time and tap Add.

Where it stops working. When the time is used, iOS covers the app with a button that offers one more minute, a reminder in 15 minutes, or Ignore Limit For Today. One tap, and the limit is gone. How to make a limit hold has the fix.

5. Give the evening a closing time

Late evening is when most people scroll longest. Downtime closes the apps you choose between two times, every night.

  1. In Settings, tap Screen Time, then Downtime.
  2. Turn on Scheduled and set the hours.
  3. Go back and open Always Allowed to choose what stays open, such as Phone, Messages and your alarm.

The steps for making this one firm are in How to block apps at night.

6. Decide what you will do instead

Closing an app leaves a gap, and the gap gets filled by the next app unless something else is ready. Pick two or three small things that take five to ten minutes: a walk round the block, a few pages of a book, a glass of water, a message to someone you like. Put the book where the phone usually sits.

This sounds too simple to matter. It is the step people skip, and the one that makes the others last.

7. Put a pause in front of the app

Everything above either hides the app or cuts it off after the time is spent. None of it catches the moment that matters, which is the half second when you open it without deciding to.

That is what Pausely is for. You choose the apps. After that, tapping one doesn't open it straight away. You see how many times you have opened it today, you breathe for ten seconds, and then you choose: I changed my mind, or continue for a few minutes. If you go in, the pause is back when the time is up.

It doesn't lock you out, and it doesn't lecture. It turns a reflex back into a choice. Two apps and the breathing pause are free.

If you only do one thing: do number 1 tonight. It takes thirty seconds and costs nothing.
