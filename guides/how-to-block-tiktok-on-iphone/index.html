How to block TikTok on iPhone (4 ways, lightest to firmest)

TikTok lives in two places on your phone: the app, and tiktok.com in Safari. Here are four ways to block it, from a soft reminder to a block with no way round, with the steps for each and the point where it stops holding.

By the Pausely team · Updated 27 November 2026 · 6 min read

Before you start, decide what you want. If you want TikTok gone, go to option 3. If you still enjoy it and only want to stop losing whole evenings, options 1, 2 and 4 are closer.

Option 1: TikTok's own screen time limit

TikTok has a daily limit built in. Open your profile, tap the menu at the top, and go to Settings and privacy. Look for Screen time (TikTok moves this section from time to time), then Daily screen time, and set a number of minutes. The same section has break reminders and sleep reminders.

Where it stops working. When the time is up, TikTok asks for a passcode you set yourself. You know the passcode. It is a reminder, not a block.

Option 2: A Screen Time limit on the app

This uses Screen Time, which is already on your iPhone, so the limit sits outside TikTok.

  1. Open Settings and tap Screen Time. If it has never been set up, turn on App & Website Activity first.
  2. Tap App Limits, then Add Limit.
  3. Open the Social category and tick TikTok.
  4. Scroll to the bottom of the same list, open Websites, tap Add Website and type tiktok.com. Now one limit covers the app and the site together.
  5. Tap Next, set the time (the smallest is 1 minute a day) and tap Add.

Where it stops working. When the time runs out, iOS covers the app and offers one more minute, a reminder in 15 minutes, or Ignore Limit For Today. That last one is a single tap. To remove it, set a Screen Time passcode and turn on Block at End of Limit. It only really holds if someone else chooses the passcode.

Option 3: Remove it completely

Three steps: delete the app, stop it coming back, and block the website.

  1. Press and hold the TikTok icon, tap Remove App, then Delete App. Your account and your videos stay on TikTok's side.
  2. In Settings, go to Screen Time, then Content & Privacy Restrictions, and turn the switch on. Tap iTunes & App Store Purchases, then Installing Apps, and choose Don't Allow. The App Store disappears from your Home Screen until you change this back.
  3. Still in Content & Privacy Restrictions, tap Content Restrictions (on newer versions of iOS this row is called App Store, Media, Web & Games), then Web Content, and choose Limit Adult Websites. Under Never Allow, tap Add Website and enter tiktok.com.

Two side effects to know about. Limit Adult Websites also turns on Apple's adult content filter and removes Private Browsing from Safari. And with Installing Apps off you can't install anything else either, so you will be switching it back on now and then. That moment is when TikTok tends to return.

Option 4: Keep it, with a pause in front

For most people the trouble is not TikTok itself. It is opening TikTok forty times a day without deciding to. A block treats every open the same. A pause sorts the ones you meant from the ones you didn't.

With Pausely, tapping TikTok shows you how many times you have opened it today, then asks for a ten second breath. After it you choose: I changed my mind, or continue for a few minutes. When the time is up, the pause comes back.

If you want it firmer, Premium adds a daily limit that rests the app until tomorrow, rules that block it at the hours you choose, and tiktok.com in Safari.
