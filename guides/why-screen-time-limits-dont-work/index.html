Why you keep tapping Ignore Limit, and how to make a Screen Time limit hold

You set a one hour limit. The hour ends, a grey screen appears, and your thumb has tapped Ignore Limit For Today before you have read it. You are not unusually weak. The limit was built with a door in it. Here is how to close the door.

By the Pausely team · Updated 27 November 2026 · 5 min read

Why the limit gives way

A Screen Time limit does nothing at all until your time is used. Then it interrupts you at the worst possible moment, when you are mid scroll and want to continue, and offers three buttons: One More Minute, Remind Me in 15 Minutes and Ignore Limit For Today.

The person tapping that button is the tired, mid scroll version of you, and the calm version who set the limit isn't in the room. After a week the tap is automatic, and the limit has become one more thing you dismiss.

Fix 1: Take the button away

iOS can remove the ignore option, but only when Screen Time has a passcode.

  1. Open Settings and tap Screen Time.
  2. Tap Lock Screen Time Settings (older versions call it Use Screen Time Passcode) and set a four digit code.
  3. Go to App Limits, tap your limit, and turn on Block at End of Limit.

Now the cover offers Ask For More Time, which gives one more minute and then needs the passcode.

The catch. You know the passcode. Typing four digits is slower than one tap, and that helps a little, but not for long. It works properly when somebody else sets the code and doesn't tell you: a partner, a friend, a sibling. If you live alone, write it on paper and put the paper somewhere annoying to reach.

Fix 2: Make the limit smaller than you think

A two hour limit is spent by early evening, exactly when you most want the app, and that is when you override it. Try a short limit that you expect to hit early, such as 20 minutes, and treat hitting it as the signal that today's ration is done. Limits reset at midnight.

Fix 3: Use a schedule instead of a budget

Budgets need you to keep track. Schedules don't. Downtime closes the apps you choose between two times every day, such as 10 p.m. to 7 a.m. Find it in Settings, Screen Time, Downtime. With a passcode set, turn on Block at Downtime so it can't be waved away. The full steps are here.

When a limit is the wrong tool

A limit measures minutes. But for many people the trouble is not the total. It is the number of times they open the app without meaning to: in a queue, between tasks, in the middle of a sentence. Twenty opens of two minutes each stay well inside most limits, and still break up a whole day.

For that, the interruption has to come at the start, before the feed loads, when stopping is easy.

That is how Pausely works. You choose the apps. Each time you tap one you see how many times you have opened it today, and you breathe for ten seconds. Then you choose: I changed my mind, or continue for a few minutes. There is no ignore button to learn, because the pause is short enough that you don't need one.

And if you do want a hard stop as well, Premium has daily limits that rest an app until tomorrow, and a strict mode that can't be undone until the time you chose, not even by you.
