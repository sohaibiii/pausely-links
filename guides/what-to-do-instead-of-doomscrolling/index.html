What to do instead of doomscrolling: 21 ideas, sorted by how long you have

Closing the app is the easy half. The hard half is the empty minute after it, which the next app fills unless something else is already waiting. Here are 21 things to put in that minute, sorted by how much time you actually have.

By the Pausely team · Updated 28 November 2026 · 6 min read

Why "just stop" doesn't work

Scrolling usually fills a gap: waiting, boredom, tiredness, putting off something harder. If you take the phone away and leave the gap, the gap wins. So the trick is not more willpower. It is having the next thing decided before the gap arrives.

Pick two or three ideas from the lists below, one for each kind of moment you scroll in. You don't need all 21.

If you have one minute

For queues, lifts, the kettle, the gap between meetings.

  1. Breathe slowly five times. In through the nose, longer out. It is the quickest reset there is.
  2. Look at something far away. Out of a window, down the street. Your eyes have been at arm's length all day.
  3. Stand up and stretch. Neck, shoulders, the back you have been curling over the phone.
  4. Drink a glass of water. Half of the restless feeling is sometimes just thirst.
  5. Send one real message. Not a reaction. A sentence to someone you like.
  6. Write down the thing on your mind. One line on paper or in a notes app, so it stops circling.

If you have ten minutes

For breaks, the bus, the time before you start something.

  1. Walk round the block. No podcast needed. This is the single best swap on the list.
  2. Read a few pages of a book. Keep one where the phone usually sits.
  3. Tidy one small thing. A drawer, the sink, your desk. It ends with something visibly better.
  4. Listen to one album track, properly. Eyes closed, nothing else.
  5. Do a short practice. Ten minutes of a language, an instrument, a sketch.
  6. Call someone instead of watching them. Their stories, live, are better than their posts.
  7. Start the task you were avoiding, for two minutes. Often the scrolling was putting this off. Two minutes is usually enough to get past the start.

If it is the evening

For the long scroll on the sofa or in bed, which is the one that costs the most.

  1. Put the phone on charge in another room and come back without it.
  2. Watch one thing on purpose. A film or an episode you chose, on the big screen, then stop. Choosing is the difference.
  3. Cook something slightly more than you need to.
  4. Have a bath or a long shower. No phone in the bathroom.
  5. Play a game with real pieces. Cards, a puzzle, a board game, even alone.
  6. Write tomorrow's three things. It quietens the urge to check everything now.
  7. Stretch on the floor for ten minutes. It is hard to scroll lying on your back with your arms out.
  8. Go to bed early with a book. If you are scrolling because you are tired, the answer is sleep, not a better feed.

How to make the swap actually happen

Ideas on a list don't help at 11 p.m. Three small things do.

  • Decide in advance, with a when. "When I sit on the sofa after dinner, I pick up the book" works better than "I'll read more". The moment triggers the plan.
  • Put the alternative where the phone was. Book on the pillow, shoes by the door, water on the desk. Make the new thing the easy thing.
  • Add friction to the old thing. Move the app off your first Home Screen, or set a limit. Our guide on how to stop doomscrolling has seven ways, and how to block apps on iPhone covers the built-in blocks.

And be kind about slips. Missing one evening doesn't undo a week. Pick it up the next day.
