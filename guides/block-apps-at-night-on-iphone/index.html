How to block apps at night on iPhone

The last scroll of the day is the longest one, because nothing is waiting to interrupt it. Your iPhone can close chosen apps at a set time every night. Here is how to set it up so your alarm still rings and the block is still there at 11.30 p.m.

By the Pausely team · Updated 27 November 2026 · 5 min read

First: Sleep Focus doesn't block apps

Many people turn on Sleep Focus and expect their apps to close. It doesn't do that. Sleep Focus silences notifications and dims the Lock Screen. Every app still opens normally. It is worth having, and it is not a block. For a block you need Downtime.

Set up Downtime

  1. Open Settings and tap Screen Time. If it has never been set up, turn on App & Website Activity first.
  2. Tap Downtime and turn on Scheduled.
  3. Choose Every Day, or Customize Days if weekends are different, and set From and To. Starting 30 to 60 minutes before you want to be asleep works well.

During those hours every app is covered except Phone and the ones you allow in the next step. iOS gives a reminder five minutes before it starts.

Choose what stays open

  1. Go back to Screen Time and tap Always Allowed.
  2. Tap the plus beside anything you need at night: Clock for your alarm, Messages, Maps, your music or audiobook app, a meditation app.
  3. Under Contacts on the same screen, choose who can reach you during Downtime.

Be honest with this list. If you allow the browser, the feeds are all still there on the web.

Shopping apps belong off the list too if late night orders are the problem rather than the feeds. We also make On Ice, a shopping blocker, and wrote a guide on how to stop late night online shopping.

Make it hold

Out of the box, a covered app has an Ignore Limit button under it, with Ignore Limit For Today one tap further in. To remove it:

  1. In Screen Time, tap Lock Screen Time Settings (older versions call it Use Screen Time Passcode) and set a code.
  2. Go back into Downtime and turn on Block at Downtime.

Now getting past it needs the passcode. As with every Screen Time passcode, it holds best when someone else chose it.

Three small things that help more than they should

  • Charge the phone outside the bedroom. Or at least out of reach of the bed. A cheap alarm clock removes the last reason to keep it close.
  • Put something by the bed. A book, a notebook, a puzzle. The half hour needs filling with something.
  • Start Downtime earlier than feels necessary. If it begins at the moment you want to be asleep, you will meet it mid scroll and argue with it.

If Downtime is too blunt

Downtime covers everything outside your allowed list, on one schedule, and its only answer is the passcode. Some nights you do need the app for two minutes, and that is when people turn the whole thing off and forget to turn it back on.

A rule in Pausely is narrower. You pick the apps and the hours, and choose what happens when you tap one in that time: a pause first, such as ten seconds of breathing and then a choice, or a full block until the hours are over. Outside those hours the same apps can keep their ordinary pause.

One rule is free. If you want the night to be something you can't talk yourself out of, Premium has strict mode, which holds until a time you pick and can't be undone before then, not even by you.
