How to block apps on iPhone: 5 ways, from a nudge to a full block

Your iPhone can already block apps. It has four different ways to do it, hidden in Screen Time and on the Home Screen, and each one blocks something slightly different. Here they are, lightest first, with the steps and the point where each one stops holding.

By the Pausely team · Updated 28 November 2026 · 7 min read

The short answer. Open Settings, tap Screen Time, then App Limits and Add Limit. Tick the app, set 1 minute, and tap Add. To stop yourself switching it off, set a Screen Time passcode and turn on Block at End of Limit. The rest of this page explains the other ways, and which one fits.

Before you start: turn on Screen Time

Every built-in block lives in Screen Time. Open Settings and tap Screen Time. If it has never been set up, turn on App & Website Activity. It takes a few seconds and changes nothing else.

Option 1: A daily limit (App Limits)

Best when you want to keep using an app, just less.

  1. In Screen Time, tap App Limits, then Add Limit.
  2. Open a category, for example Social or Entertainment, and tick the apps. Ticking the category itself covers every app in it, including ones you install later.
  3. To cover the website version too, scroll to the bottom, open Websites, tap Add Website and type its address.
  4. Tap Next, set the time (the smallest is 1 minute a day) and tap Add.

Where it stops working. When the time is used, iOS covers the app with a screen that offers one more minute, a reminder in 15 minutes, or Ignore Limit For Today. One tap and the limit is gone for the day. How to make a limit hold shows how to remove that button.

Option 2: A schedule (Downtime)

Best when the problem has a time of day, usually late evening.

  1. In Screen Time, tap Downtime and turn on Scheduled.
  2. Choose the days and set From and To.
  3. Go back and open Always Allowed to choose what stays open, such as Messages, Maps and Clock for your alarm.

During those hours every app outside the allowed list is covered. Where it stops working: the same Ignore Limit button appears, and Downtime covers everything at once, so one urgent app at 11 p.m. tempts you to turn the whole thing off. The full set-up is in How to block apps at night.

Option 3: Hide apps completely (Content & Privacy Restrictions)

Best when you want an app gone, with no button to bring it back. This is the strongest block iOS has, and the bluntest.

For Apple's own apps, such as Safari, Camera or FaceTime:

  1. In Screen Time, tap Content & Privacy Restrictions and turn the switch on.
  2. Tap Allowed Apps (on newer versions of iOS it is Allowed Apps & Features) and turn off the ones you want hidden.

They vanish from the Home Screen and the App Library until you turn them back on.

For apps from the App Store, iOS can only hide them all together. In Content & Privacy Restrictions, tap Content Restrictions (newer versions call it App Store, Media, Web & Games), then Apps, and choose Don't Allow Apps. Every app you installed disappears, which is rarely what an adult wants. To remove one app and keep it away, delete it and turn off installing instead: in Content & Privacy Restrictions, tap iTunes & App Store Purchases, then Installing Apps, and choose Don't Allow.

Where it stops working. Anyone who knows the Screen Time passcode can undo it in seconds, and with installing switched off you will be switching it back on every time you need a new app. That moment is when the old app tends to come back.

Option 4: Lock an app with Face ID

On iOS 18 and later, press and hold an app's icon and tap Require Face ID. You can also choose Hide and Require Face ID, which moves it into a hidden folder at the bottom of the App Library.

This is a privacy feature, not a block. It stops other people opening the app. It doesn't stop you, because your face opens it in under a second. Hiding the icon helps a little, for the same reason moving an icon off your first Home Screen helps: your thumb has further to go.

Option 5: A pause before the app opens

Options 1 to 3 either cut you off after the time is spent or remove the app altogether. Most of the time neither is what you want. You want to keep the app for the things it is good for, and stop opening it on autopilot.

That is the moment Pausely works on. You choose the apps. Tap one and it doesn't open straight away: you see how many times you have opened it today, you breathe for ten seconds, and then you choose. I changed my mind closes it. Continuing gives you a few minutes, and when they are up the pause comes back.

If you want something firmer, a rule in Pausely can block chosen apps during the hours you set, and Premium adds a daily limit that rests an app until tomorrow and a strict mode that can't be switched off early, not even by you.
